    STEP 4 — Have the players make their AI decisions

    For you · Facilitate

    Ask players to choose Human, AI, or Human + AI for each of the 20 tasks on their boards. Be ready to clarify unfamiliar tasks using the task glossary.

    Encourage individual decisions, but pair students so they can talk through their choices with a peer. Each player should still complete their own board.

    Task glossary

    Human means the task is done by a person. AI means the task is automated. Human + AI means a person works with AI and reviews its output.

    STEP 6 — Report their Reach and Trust

    For you · Facilitate

    First read the explanation below. Then tell each player their Reach and Trust from the table, including why the game assigned those levels. Give everyone time to respond before applying the story effects in Step 7.

    Read aloud · Explain the outcomes

    “Your decisions about how much AI to use affect two outcomes: Reach and Trust. Reach means how widely your coverage gets to an audience. Trust means how much confidence readers have in your coverage.

    In this game, greater AI dependence raises Reach and lowers Trust. The idea is that automation can help a newsroom produce, adapt, and distribute more coverage, while human judgment, verification, and attention to audiences can help sustain confidence in it.

    These are assumptions built into the game, not predictions about every newsroom. Let’s look at the results of your choices and discuss whether those assumptions make sense.

    Every story type is different, too. Readers may be comfortable with AI doing a task in one kind of story, but expect more human involvement in the same task for another kind. After we discuss these results, we’ll look at three tasks that matter especially for this story.”

    STEP 7 — Calculate story bonuses and penalties

    For you · Facilitate

    Ask each player what they chose for the three tasks below. These tasks matter for this particular story. Enter their choices, then tell them how their Reach and Trust changed.

    Read aloud · Introduce story effects

    “Now let’s consider this particular story. The same AI choice can have different consequences depending on what readers need. For each of these three tasks, tell me whether you chose Human, AI, or Human + AI.”

      Discuss
      • Which story effect changed your outcome? Does it fit this story?
      • Why might AI help with one of these tasks but create problems with another?
      • Would you change any of your decisions now? Why or why not?
      Story effects and each player’s calculation

      Each matching effect moves the named outcome one level, in printed order. This calculator keeps outcomes between Very low and Very high.

      STEP 8 — Main takeaways: What did we learn?

      For you · Facilitate

      Invite players to identify what they learned before offering a summary. Ask them to connect a takeaway to a specific decision they made. Use the ideas below to support the discussion; players may question the game’s assumptions.

      Reflect together
      • Which decision would you reconsider, and what did the exercise reveal about it?
      • How did the story’s topic and audience needs change your view of an AI task?
      • Which assumption about Reach or Trust would you question? What evidence would help you assess it?
      • What would you want to know before using AI for a real newsroom task?
      Read aloud · Bring the ideas together

      “There is no single AI choice that fits every newsroom task. Our choices depend on the task, the story, and what readers need.

      Reaching more people and earning their trust are different goals. Human involvement also matters: reviewing an AI output means making judgments about its accuracy, fairness, and usefulness.

      The game makes these trade-offs visible through rules. Those rules are assumptions we can discuss and challenge, rather than evidence that AI always increases Reach or reduces Trust.”

      Task and context matter

      Consider the specific task, the story, and the audience before choosing an approach.

      Reach and Trust are different

      Getting coverage to more people does not automatically make it credible.

      Human review is active work

      Checking evidence and making editorial judgments require more than simply accepting an AI output.

      Models contain assumptions

      Ask what a rule represents, what it leaves out, and how you would test it.
