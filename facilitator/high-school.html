    STEP 4 — Have the players make their AI decisions

    For you · What to do

    Ask students to choose Human, AI, or Human + AI for all 20 tasks. If a task is unclear, use the task glossary.

    Have students make their own choices. Put them in pairs so they can explain their choices to a classmate. Each student fills out their own board.

    Task glossary

    Human means a person does the task. AI means AI does it on its own. Human + AI means a person uses AI and checks its work.

    STEP 6 — Report their Reach and Trust

    For you · What to do

    Read the explanation below. Tell each student their Reach and Trust, and use the last column to explain the scores. Let them talk about the results before going to Step 7.

    Read aloud · Explain the outcomes

    “Your AI choices affect two scores: Reach and Trust. Reach means how many people your news gets to. Trust means how much readers believe they can rely on your news.

    In this game, relying more on AI raises Reach and lowers Trust. AI can help make and share more news quickly. People can help readers trust the news by checking facts, noticing what is missing, and answering questions.

    That is how this game works. Real news is more complicated: AI does not always raise Reach or lower Trust. Let’s look at your scores and talk about whether the game’s rules make sense.

    Stories are different, too. Readers might be fine with AI doing a task for one story, but want a person involved for another. Next, we’ll look at three tasks that are important for this story.”

    STEP 7 — Calculate story bonuses and penalties

    For you · What to do

    Ask each student what they chose for these three tasks. Enter their answers. Show them what changed in their Reach and Trust.

    Read aloud · Introduce story effects

    “Now think about what readers need from this story. The same AI choice can work differently in different stories. For these three tasks, did you choose Human, AI, or Human + AI?”

      Discuss
      • Which task changed your score? Does that change make sense for this story?
      • Why could AI help with one task but cause a problem with another?
      • Would you change any choices now? Why?
      Why each student’s scores changed

      When a story rule matches a choice, Reach or Trust moves up or down one level. The rules run in the order shown. Scores stay between Very low and Very high.

      STEP 8 — Main takeaways: What did we learn?

      For you · What to do

      Ask students what they learned before reading the summary. Have each student give one example from their own board. It is okay to disagree with a game rule—ask them to explain why.

      Reflect together
      • Which choice would you change? What helped you see it differently?
      • Would you use AI differently for another story? Give an example.
      • Which game rule would you question? What would you need to find out to check it?
      • What would you check before using AI to help make real news?
      Read aloud · Bring the ideas together

      “There is no one AI choice that works for every task. Think about the job, the story, and what readers need.

      Getting news to more people is different from getting them to trust it. A person working with AI still needs to check the facts, look for missing voices, and ask whether the work helps readers.

      The game’s rules help us talk about these choices. But the game is simpler than real life. Its scores do not prove that AI always helps news spread or makes people trust it less.”

      Think about the task and story

      Ask: What is the job? What is the story? What do readers need?

      Being seen is not the same as being trusted

      Lots of people seeing news does not mean they can rely on it.

      People still need to check the work

      Using AI with a person means checking and improving its work, not just saying “looks good.”

      The game is simpler than real life

      Ask what the rules help us see, what they leave out, and how we could check them.
