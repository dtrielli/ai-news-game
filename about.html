About the AI News Game

Return to home

The AI News Game is a newsroom simulation about the choices involved in using artificial intelligence to produce and distribute journalism. It invites players to consider AI use task by task, rather than as one decision for an entire newsroom.

How the game works

Players decide whether Human, AI, or Human + AI will perform 20 newsroom tasks. The main digital game presents one story on each of three days. An expanded edition presents three stories per day. Story type changes how some AI-assisted decisions affect reach and trust.

1 · Make assignmentsChoose who performs each reporting, editing, packaging, and audience task.
2 · Observe trade-offsSee how the full editorial plan affects estimated reach and trust.
3 · Follow the audienceSee how reach attracts visitors and trust contributes to repeat visits and subscribers.

Why a game?

Games can turn abstract ideas into a concrete, practical understanding of social mechanics. This game shows players that using AI in journalism is not one decision, but a series of editorial choices. Those choices can shape how audiences perceive journalistic work and how they choose to engage with it.

A playtest model

The numerical effects are provisional game mechanics. They are intended to make trade-offs visible during playtesting; they are not empirical claims about the performance of people or AI.

Privacy

Player choices and saved progress remain in the player’s browser and are not transmitted. This site uses cookie-free Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits and understand which pages are used. It does not track gameplay decisions.

The optional feedback form is hosted by Google and opens separately from the game. Information is sent to Google only if a player chooses to submit the form.

Classroom use

The AI News Game can be played individually or used in class to explore how AI choices affect journalism and its audiences.

For classroom sessions, players make decisions on pen-and-paper boards while a facilitator leads the discussion. The online facilitator guide includes story introductions, questions, a task glossary, and a calculator for up to eight players. Choose the college-level or high-school guide; both use the same rules, with language and prompts suited to each group.

The printable materials include a player board and 10 facilitator cards. A separate cards-and-chips edition remains in development.

This is a playtest version. The scoring model, story scenarios, classroom discussion prompts, and print-and-play materials are still being developed and tested.
